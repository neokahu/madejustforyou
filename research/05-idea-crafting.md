# Stage 2 (deep) — The Idea Engine: crafting a design, not copying one

> **The trap this fixes:** demand data (search volume, Etsy carts, TikTok plays) only proves a
> topic is *wanted*. It cannot hand you a design — the best it can hand you is *what already
> exists*. Let the data generate ideas and you've re-invented competitor-spying. This stage is
> the **generative logic** that turns a validated slot into an original, sellable concept.

Demand data has exactly two jobs here, and neither is "give me the idea":
1. **Filter** — which slots are worth crafting for (Stage 1 + 3).
2. **Vocabulary source** — the raw *language* to recombine (see "The bridge" below).

---

## The idea equation

```
VALIDATED SLOT   →   MECHANIC   +   ANGLE   +   HOOK   +   AESTHETIC   →   PRODUCT
 (from demand)       (the lever)   (emotion)   (words)   (current look)
```

The slot comes from the data. **The idea is the four middle choices.** Craft them in order —
the Mechanic first, because it does the most work.

---

## 1. The Personalization Mechanic — the real idea engine

A **mechanic** is a *rule that turns the buyer's real life into the artwork*. It is the
POD-specific lever most people miss, and it is what makes a design ownable and repeatable. The
strongest mechanics **scale or vary per buyer**.

| Mechanic | Rule | Why it works | Re-skins as |
|----------|------|-------------|-------------|
| **Roster** | "one X per [person]" | grows with the family; every unit is personal | flower / star / bird / bee / heart / handprint per grandchild |
| **Handwriting** | use their *actual* hand | it's literally them — max emotion | recipe, child's drawing, signature |
| **Role transition** | "was X → now Y" | names a life milestone | First Dad→Now Grandpa; Promoted to Grandma |
| **Data-to-art** | encode a fact visually | feels custom + designed | birth stats, map/coordinates, star map, Spotify code |
| **Possession/belonging** | "belongs to [names]" | ties the object to the tribe | This Grandpa belongs to…; Grandma's Crew |

> **Key:** the birth-flower winner isn't about *flowers* — it's the **Roster mechanic**
> ("one-per-grandchild"). Identify the mechanic under any winner and you hold the reusable
> engine, not a single design. A proven mechanic **ports to new recipients and occasions** —
> that's how ideas compound instead of being one-offs.

## 2. The Emotional Angle — why it lands as a gift
sentimental · funny · memorial · milestone · identity · nostalgic. The same product + recipient
flips completely on angle. Pick the angle deliberately; it sets the hook's tone.

## 3. The Hook — how to actually write the line
The hook is the crafted phrase/concept. Generate candidates from patterns:
- **Possessive:** "Grandma's Garden", "Grandpa's Grilling Crew"
- **Identity claim:** "In a world full of grandmas, be a Mimi"
- **Metaphor map:** family = garden / night sky / flock / hive / tree
- **Reappropriated idiom:** "No place like Grandma's house"
- **Number brag:** "Blessed with 5 grandkids"
- **Role/transition:** "First Dad, Now Grandpa"

## 4. The Aesthetic — the current visual language
watercolor botanical · retro/groovy type · embroidered/Comfort-Colors · minimalist line art.
**This is what you pull from Pinterest** — it's what keeps a design from looking dated. The same
hook in last year's clip-art vs this year's aesthetic is the difference between a flop and a
scroll-stopper.

---

## The bridge: from "detect" to "create"

You **mine language, not products.** From Etsy titles, TikTok captions, and reviews, extract the
*vocabulary*, then recombine it through a Mechanic + Hook pattern into something new:

| Extract from the data | Feeds |
|-----------------------|-------|
| Nicknames (Gigi, Nana, Mimi, Poppy, Abuela) | variants, hook |
| Metaphors people use (garden, crew, era) | hook, aesthetic |
| Exact emotional phrasing (from reviews/captions) | hook, ad copy |
| Which mechanic underlies the top sellers | your engine |
| The current aesthetic (from Pinterest) | look |

**Steal the dictionary, not the sentence.**

---

## Differentiation — 5 moves so it's crafted, not copied

1. **Same mechanic, new metaphor** — "one flower per grandchild" → "one **bird** per grandchild"
   (*Nana's Little Birds*), "one **star**" (*Grandma's Little Stars*). Proven mechanic, fresh design.
2. **Same hook, current aesthetic** — their clip-art → your warm watercolor.
3. **Stack two mechanics** — photo + handwriting.
4. **Proven angle → under-served recipient** — birth-flower is saturated for grandma → apply to
   *godmother*, *dog-mom's pack*, *teacher's students*.
5. **White-space** — high search demand + weak/generic existing execution = craft the better one.

> Against a competitor who *owns* a design (e.g. macorner + birth-flower garden): take their
> **mechanic** to a metaphor they don't own, in your brand's aesthetic. Same validated demand,
> original product.

## Worked example — crafting from a validated slot

**Slot (validated in the GPD sprint):** grandma × Grandparents Day × birth-flower theme.
Instead of copying the birth-flower garden listing:

1. **Mechanic:** Roster — "one _____ per grandchild" (the real engine under the winner).
2. **Angle:** sentimental pride — "look how my family grew."
3. **Hook + metaphor swap (differentiation move #1):**
   - *Grandma's Garden* → a birth flower per grandchild (the known lane — do one, but…)
   - *Grandma's Little Birds* → a bird per grandchild on a wire
   - *Grandma's Night Sky* → a star per grandchild
   - *Grandma's Hive* → a bee per grandchild, "the whole hive loves you"
   Four distinct designs, one proven mechanic — none a copy.
4. **Aesthetic:** warm watercolor botanical (Pinterest-validated) in the brand palette.
5. **Product:** sweatshirt (AOV) + mug (cheap Tier-1 test).

Each output is a briefable design (→ [`templates/design-brief.md`](templates/design-brief.md)) and a
row for the Idea Database — generated by *logic*, validated by *demand*, not lifted from a rival.

> **Standard step — link your sources (principle #7).** Every concept must cite the live
> listings / posts / ads it was crafted from. End each sprint's concept doc with a
> **"Reference examples"** section grouped by concept, so the design is inspectable and the
> differentiation move is provable (we crafted *from* it, not *copied* it).

## Where AI image-gen fits
The tools (nano-banana / imagen / ideogram) are the **renderer, not the ideator.** You bring the
Mechanic + Angle + Hook + Aesthetic; the model produces concept mockups fast for Tier-1 tests.
Prompt quality = how precisely you specify those four choices.
