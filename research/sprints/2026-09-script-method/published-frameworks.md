# Published frameworks for short-form video ad scripts (Meta, e-commerce)

Researched 2026-09-26. Sources: Tavily web search and extraction. WinningHunter was not used.
Scope: evidence-backed rules for writing 9:16 short-form video ad scripts for a personalized-gift Shopify store on Meta.

**How to read this.** Grade **A** = the platform's own guidance, or a study with a stated sample and method. **B** = an industry dataset with a stated n, but proprietary or correlational. **C** = practitioner opinion, or a blog restating numbers with no traceable source. Items marked **[not re-verified]** are well-known published findings that were cited from their known source but not re-opened in this pass. Check them before quoting them externally.

## 2. Rules

### 2.1 Hook (first 1–3 s)
- **H1. Put the main message in the first 3 seconds.** Meta's mobile-first guidance: "Get your main message across in the first 3 seconds" and "start bold". [S1, A] Facebook IQ found most campaign value lands early (~47% in 3s) [S3, A, not re-verified]. TikTok: 90% of ad-recall impact comes in the first 6 seconds [S10, A].
- **H2. The opening should carry motion and change.** "Ads with more motion, scenes and speed tend to be more relevant and interesting." [S1, A] ABCD Attention also says to drop viewers straight in and immerse them. [S7, A]
- **H3. Don't open on a logo card or a slow intro.** This is implied by S1 and S7. Most practitioner guides say it explicitly. [S14, C]
- **H4. Clarity beats cleverness when the goal is conversion.** Motion found price, offer or tension hooks surface winners more often than vague lifestyle openings. "Offer only" had a hit rate of about 9.3%, and storytelling is also on the list. [S11, B] This is correlational and depends on the vertical.
- **H5. Emotion, suspense and surprise are valid hook types.** TikTok names "suspense, surprise, and emotion". [S10, A] A narrative (Thai-film) opening is compatible with platform guidance as long as it is visually arresting in second 1.

### 2.2 Structure / beats
- **S1. Use three parts: hook, then body, then close with a CTA.** TikTok's official recommendation. [S10, A] Meta's Reels team describes beginning, middle and payoff (via Celtra). [S5, B]
- **S2. Use one message per ad.** Meta: "clear, concise, single-minded messages". [S1, A]
- **S3. The ABCD checklist is the only structure framework here backed by outcome data** (n = 11,000; +30% short-term sales likelihood). [S7/S8, A] Caveat: the data is YouTube, not Meta.
- **S4. PAS, AIDA and hook-body-CTA templates are opinion.** [S15, C] They fit S1 but have no evidence of their own.

### 2.3 Pacing & length
- **P1. Meta's official guidance says 15 seconds or less.** [S1, A]
- **P2. More scenes and speed tend to help** (Meta [S1, A]). TikTok "Stimulation" guidance says the same. [S10, A]
- **P3. Practitioner length claims:** 6–15s for cold audiences, 15–30s for balance, 21–34s for TikTok story ads, and 60s+ for warm audiences or deep storytelling. [S14, C] There is no primary data for these numbers.

### 2.4 Product / brand reveal timing
- **R1. Official guidance says show the brand or product early.** Meta: "Consider showing brand or product imagery in the first few seconds" and "highlight your brand early and often". [S2, S1, A] ABCD Branding says to brand early (in the first 5s) and often. [S7, A] TikTok: product on screen gives +65% brand affinity and +25% recall. [S10, A] System1: Spike rises when the brand is identified quickly. [S13, B]
- **R2. This conflicts with a late-reveal story format** (see section 3).

### 2.5 Sound-off / captions
- **C1. The ad must work with sound off.** Meta: videos "should not rely on sound to tell your brand's story" but should "delight if sound is on". [S1, A] Feed video must be "understandable regardless of sound by using captions". [S3, A] Use burned-in captions and text.
- **C2. Reels needs audio too.** Meta Reels rule 2 is to build with audio [S4, A], which gave +15% average positive response score [S5, B]. The platforms say "design for both", not "silent only".
- **C3. Keep text inside the safe zones.** Meta rule 3, with +39% CTR. [S4 A / S5 B]
- **C4. Treat the "85% watched silently" figure as unreliable.** It traces back to a 2016 Digiday estimate and is repeated without a source. [S14, C]

### 2.6 Emotional vs direct response
- **E1. Emotion drives the long-term effect.** In the IPA databank, emotion-led campaigns outperform rational ones on large business effects, especially over long horizons. [S12, A, not re-verified] System1 argues for "pure emotion" over "double duty" ads. [S13, B]
- **E2. DR-style signals also win short-term on Meta.** Motion: offer, price and urgency hooks show high hit rates. [S11, B] Google/Nielsen found a relevant CTA and offer was the single strongest ROI attribute. [S9, B]
- **E3. The practical reconciliation is to lead with emotion and close with direction.** ABCD covers both: Connection for emotion and Direction for the CTA and offer. [S7, A] This is a synthesis, not a tested claim.

### 2.7 Proof
- **PR1. Show the product in context and in use.** Nielsen/Google ranked "product in context" among its three golden rules. [S9, B] TikTok's data on the product being on screen supports this. [S10, A]
- **PR2.** No A/B-grade source was found for reviews or testimonials inside video scripts. Treat them as practitioner opinion. [S15, C]

### 2.8 CTA
- **CTA1. Always close with a clear CTA tied to the product or offer.** A relevant CTA gave +2.6pp ROI, the top Direction attribute. [S9, B] ABCD Direction. [S7, A] TikTok CTA cards gave +45% recall and +19% likeability, and TikTok advises using a card rather than interrupting the content. [S10, A]
- **CTA2.** Discounts, offers and urgency strengthen Direction. [S9, B]

### 2.9 Format
- **F1. Use 9:16 vertical as the master format.** This is Meta Reels rule 1 [S4, A], and Meta reports -48% CPA from vertical [S5, B]. Crop to 4:5 for Feed if needed.
- **F2. Include human presence and text stickers.** Meta studies found these ads earned 2x more Reels placements (via Celtra). [S5, B]

## 3. Conflicts

1. **Early brand/product vs late reveal.** Meta, Google ABCD, TikTok and System1 all reward early brand and product presence (R1). The house style (Thai emotional film, with the product as a late payoff) runs against every A-grade source. Possible compromise, which is synthesis and untested: brand or product cues in the first 3–5s, such as a logo bug, a glimpse of the product or the gift box, while the full emotional reveal stays late. A/B test late reveal against an early product glimpse.
2. **Length.** Meta says 15 seconds or less [A]. Emotional storytelling and practitioner sources allow 30–60s+ [C]. Binet & Field's emotional-effectiveness evidence comes mostly from long-form or TV. Nothing A-grade says long emotional films win on Meta prospecting. Cut a 15s version as well as the long film.
3. **Sound-off vs sound-on.** Older Feed guidance says design for sound off [S1–S3]. Reels guidance says build with audio [S4/S5]. Resolution: build for both, with captions and meaningful audio.
4. **Emotion vs offer hooks.** IPA and System1 say emotion wins long-term. Motion's Meta data shows offer and clarity hooks surfacing winners short-term. Different time horizons explain much of this.
5. **Polished vs native/UGC.** Practitioners claim UGC beats polished by 25–40% [C]. No A/B source quantifies this for Meta. Motion notes that simple assets win partly because they allow faster iteration [B].
6. **Cross-platform transfer.** ABCD (YouTube) and TikTok figures are A-grade on their own platforms but only directional for Meta.

## 4. Emotional storytelling, gifts, and 55+ audiences

- **Emotional storytelling:** the strongest support is Binet & Field [A, not re-verified] and System1 [B]. Both measure brand and long-term effects, and neither isolates Meta direct-response performance. TikTok lists emotion as a valid hook [A]. ABCD Connection says to make people feel something and to use relatable characters [A].
- **Gift purchases:** no A/B-grade source specific to gift purchases was found. The in-house rule to show the family connection (see memory) remains an internal hypothesis. It is consistent with ABCD's "Connection" principle and Meta's "be relatable" and human-presence guidance, but neither source tests it.
- **Older (55+) audience:** no evidence-backed source on video-ad scripting for audiences aged 55+ surfaced in this pass. It is an open gap. Captions and legible, larger text (from the sound-off rules) are the only transferable A-grade guidance. Anything more, such as slower pacing for older viewers, would be opinion. Worth a dedicated search (AARP Media, Nielsen age-cohort studies, Meta's own 55+ insights).

## 5. Meta Personal Attributes policy (applies to on-screen text, VO and captions)

Source: S6 [A]. Meta doesn't allow ads that "assert or imply personal attributes" of the viewer: race, ethnicity, religion, beliefs, **age**, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, physical or mental health, **financial status**, voting status, criminal record, trade-union membership. The policy covers implied as well as explicit statements. Meta's worked age example fails because it "implies knowledge of the exact age of the person who sees the ad" ("people like you who just turned 62"). The same logic applies to spoken VO and burned-in captions, because those are ad content too.

What this means for scripts (synthesis applying S6; practitioner notes marked C):
- Avoid second-person attribute claims such as "Are you a new grandma?", "You're over 60…", "Grieving your mom?" or "You, a proud grandpa…". Each asserts the viewer's age, family status or health or bereavement.
- Safe patterns describe the product or a third party, not the viewer: "The gift grandmas cry over", "Made for Grandma, with every grandkid's name", or "Nana, 72, opens her gift" when a character in the story says it.
- A character talking about themselves on screen is lower risk than copy that labels the viewer [C, practitioner reading of S6].
- Bereavement or memorial gift ads are the highest-risk area, because a health or loss implication aimed at the viewer can trigger enforcement. Describe the product: "A keepsake to remember Dad".
