# Framed question
## Question to the council
Should we revise the Phase-1 dog-memorial suncatcher shooting script per our new "script method" rules (§4a) before generating — and if so, how far? Specifically: does Hook 2 keep its deliberate product-absent opening, or does the product go in frame from second 1?

## Business context
- MadeJustForYou: small Shopify print-on-demand personalized-gift store, solo operator + Claude. Ads on Meta (FB/IG). All video is AI-generated (Seedance 2.0 via AtlasCloud, ~$1 per 5s clip), product artwork composited in post (never drawn by the model).
- Product: 6-inch stained-glass-style suncatcher panel — a dog silhouette cut to the chosen breed (German Shepherd) with the dog's name ("Alex") in white script; sunlight casts coloured light + the dog-shaped shadow on the wall. $26.95, gross profit $22.34, break-even ROAS 1.21×, target CPA $15.64. Memorial / sympathy gift.
- Track record: our only live product ad (MJ4U-111 candle warmer) has run since 2026-08-09 with 0 purchases. Its creative was a slow "Thai emotional short film" with late product reveal, uniform 5s clips + crossfades; it scored hook-3s motion 3.59 vs 18.05 for a long-running competitor winner. Separate site issue: cart→checkout cliff.
- Owner's stated creative preference (memory): story-driven Thai-ad-style emotional films, product as late plot payoff; ads must show family/relationship connection, never a lonely giftee.

## The current script (not yet approved; nothing generated yet except tests)
6 ads = 3 bodies × 2 hooks, 20s each, 9:16, music bed no VO, burned captions, CTA "Make one that's only theirs", 12 AI clips ≈ $16 total.
- HOOK-1 (0–2s): moving push-in on the panel in the window, name legible. Caption: "That's not a generic dog. / That's his actual breed — and his name."
- HOOK-2 (0–2s): lateral track past an EMPTY window, product deliberately absent. Caption: "We still leave the window open for him." The script's own pre-flight says: "Hook 2 trades 3-second clarity for emotional pull… Do not 'fix' Hook 2 by putting the product in it — that deletes the experiment."
- Same two hook files are reused in front of all three bodies (controlled variable).
- Body A (name is the point): A-2 dog bowl + framed photo of owner with living dog (2–6s, no product) → A-3 reveal: coloured light + dog shadow on wall (6–11s) → A-4 owner (older man) raises hand into the light, long exhale (11–16s) → shared close on panel + end card (16–20s).
- Body B (reaction): B-2 owner washing a cup, photo behind (2–6s) → B-3 held on his FACE as coloured light crosses it, product out of frame by design (6–11s) → B-4 pull back, he turns toward the small panel (11–16s) → shared close.
- Body C (buyer swaps): C-2 a woman friend reads her phone ("Her dog died on Tuesday.") → C-3 at a florist, can't choose ("Flowers felt wrong. They die too.") → C-4 her hands turn the panel face-up in tissue, name legible ("His breed. His name. Not a generic sympathy card.") at 11s → shared close.
- Beat sheet "5s-native": shots at 2/4/5/5/4s.
- Known bug: script describes the owner in a "grey sweater"; the approved 8-image character reference set wears a grey-green plaid flannel over a white tee.

## The script-method rules (written 2026-09-26, from evidence) and their §4a proposed edits
Evidence base: 3 niche winners (grandparent keepsake ads, verified long-running in Meta Ad Library), 16 general winners (longevity NOT verified — WinningHunter days-live proved unreliable), published frameworks (Meta, Google ABCD, TikTok), n=1 motion benchmark. Survivorship bias acknowledged. ZERO story-film ads exist in either winner sample.
- R1 STRONG: personalized product on screen by 1s (niche 3/3, gift 8/8 median 0.15s; Meta/ABCD/TikTok A-grade guidance).
- R2 STRONG: personalization (name) is the hero visual for much of runtime.
- R4 MODERATE: opening shot moves. R11 MODERATE: vary shot length 2–5s, hard cuts, no uniform 5s, no crossfades (gift ads ~4.5s/shot median). R12 WEAK: closing frame moves, no frozen end card.
- R16 MODERATE: faces optional; hands + product is proven. R17 house rule: show connection.
- R20 MODERATE: story-film/late reveal is NOT the cold default — only a test cell against a product-hero control.
- §3 T1: "late reveal vs product in first second" should be its own A/B test cell (same footage), possibly a cheap middle: product glimpse 0–3s with full reveal later.
§4a proposed edits: (1) HOOK-2: hang the panel in the window from frame 1, softly in focus, keep the caption — so the hook pair tests caption angle only; product-timing moves to a separate T1 cell. (2) Body C: keep product present in C-2/C-3 (product page on her phone, or cutaway to panel in owner's window) — currently product absent 2–11s. (3) Body B: open on product, face reacts to it, instead of holding on the face before the product. (4) Re-time to varied shot lengths, e.g. 1.5/2.5/3.5/3.5/4.5/4.5, generate at 5s and trim. (5) "Reveal at 30–55%" → "gift identifiable 0–1s; name/breed payoff mid-film". (6) Hard cuts only in assembly. (7) Shared close: text + small logo over moving panel, no static card. (8) optional CTA "Make his today".

## What's at stake
~$16–20 of generation plus a test budget of ad spend; more importantly, this is the first test of the product and the store needs a first winning creative. Wrong structure = wasted spend and a misleading read on whether the product/concepts work. Also the owner's taste (Thai emotional film) vs evidence (product-hero) tension.

# Chairman synthesis
## Where the Council Agrees
**Product in frame from second 1 — unanimous.** All five advisors said so; none defended Hook 2's empty window. Reasons that came up more than once: it repeats the MJ4U-111 late-reveal pattern (hook-3s motion 3.59 vs 18.05 for the competitor winner); the experiment can't be read because Meta starves the weak hook within hours; a cold viewer reads "We still leave the window open for him" with no object as a dead person or a funeral-home ad; and putting the caption over a glowing named panel keeps the emotional line and adds the "I could buy that" moment.

**Body C is the strongest body** (4 of 5). It is the only body that shows the buyer's moment; "Flowers felt wrong. They die too." is the purchase trigger. The product still shows up too late (11s) and needs to appear early, on her phone.

**Six cells is too many** (Executor, Contrarian, First Principles). About $45–50 per cell before any cell tells you anything, so roughly $300 just to pick a hook.

**Fix the wardrobe bug** — the script says "grey sweater"; the approved reference set wears plaid flannel. Unanimous.

## Where the Council Clashes
**Keep Body B or cut it.** The Executor keeps B as the one cell that follows the owner's taste; re-opening it on the product would be a rewrite. The Contrarian and First Principles cut both A and B, because an older man alone with a dog bowl is the lonely-giftee image rule R17 bans. The disagreement is whether to keep the owner's taste inside the test or set it aside.

**More cells or fewer.** The Expansionist wants cheap breed-swap variants, a signature loop opener and new channels; everyone else wants fewer. The Expansionist is right about the long-term asset, but variant volume only finds winners once the funnel converts, and this one doesn't yet.

**Where "softly in focus" lands.** The Contrarian says §4a's own fix is a trap: a 6-inch panel softly in focus in a window reads as a coloured smudge at phone size. It passes R1 on paper but fails in the feed. "Alex" has to be readable at scroll speed, which means a close shot.

## Blind Spots the Council Caught
- **Checkout may be the real problem.** The cart→checkout cliff could zero every cell whatever the creative does. MJ4U-111's 0 purchases are confounded by that cliff, so only its hook-motion gap counts as evidence against late reveal.
- **No kill or success criteria.** There is no spend cap per cell, no metric and no read window. Read on add-to-cart rate.
- **A near-free static control.** A static product-hero image ad costs about $0 to generate and would show whether the product sells before any film is made.
- **Body C needs a new character.** The woman friend (REF-GIVER) has no locked reference set, so the turntable gate adds cost and time, while most of the approved owner set would go unused.
- **Delivery time.** "Her dog died on Tuesday" is urgent, but print-on-demand takes 1–2+ weeks. If the copy and product page don't state arrival time, people drop at shipping, and it looks like the existing checkout cliff.
- **Who actually buys is unverified.** "The friend buys it" is assumed; pet memorials are often bought by the owner, Meta can't target "friend's dog just died", and the niche evidence comes from grandparent keepsakes, not pet memorials.
- **The handover is never shown.** No body shows the friend giving the panel or the owner receiving it, so R17's connection is still not on screen.

## The Recommendation
Revise, and go further than §4a. Cut Hook 2's empty window completely; the late-reveal test (T1) waits until a control has produced sales. Launch two video cells plus a static control:
1. **Body C, rebuilt.** Open on the panel, close enough that "Alex" is readable. Then the phone, the florist and the gift, ending on the handover.
2. **A product-hero cut** made from Body A's light-on-wall payoff.
3. **A static image ad** as the baseline.

Use hard cuts and varied shot lengths, and end on the moving panel. Name the object in the call to action ("Make their dog's suncatcher") and add delivery time to the copy. Before launch, write down the spend cap per cell, and read on add-to-cart rate until checkout is proven. Body B is parked, not killed. The owner's Thai-film taste goes in the payoff beat, not the opening.
