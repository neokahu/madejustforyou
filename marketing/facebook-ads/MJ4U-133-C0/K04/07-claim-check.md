## 1. H34 claim check

### Evidence (product truth)
- **F6** (product.json): per person = skin tone, hairstyle, hair colour, eyes, glasses, sweater or T-shirt, accessories, a drink. **No mouth, smile or expression option.** Nothing in F1–F10 says the print is made from a photo.
- **Render, measured by eye at full size:** all three people (Grandpa, Grandma, Olivia) carry the **same template mouth**: an open, upper-teeth-showing smile of the same shape, size and position, with the same large brown eye template. Grandma's and Olivia's smiles are indistinguishable from Grandpa's. Rosie (dog) has a different, breed-template open mouth. The grin is therefore the art style's default, applied to every figure, not something matched to the person.
- **"Same glasses":** supported. Grandpa's figure wears circle wire glasses; glasses are an F6 choice. PASS on its own.
- **"Same grin":** not supported. The split screen (photo grin left, printed grin right, snap-zoom between them) presents the grin as a reproduced likeness. A buyer infers the ornament reproduces their grandfather's own smile, which the product cannot do; every customer's figure gets the same stock smile. Our staged photo prop only "matches" because 05 would cast the prop to the template.
- **"Same Grandpa":** after "Same grin", it completes a likeness claim (the person, not his attributes). 03 §1 asked whether "Same Grandpa" reads as matching attributes; with "Same grin" before it, it does not.

### Criteria
- **E43** (HARD, S1): "no … claims outside F1–F10". A facial-likeness or expression-matching capability is outside F6. **FAIL.**
- **P2 / E40** (product as shipped, Meta "accurately represent"): the hook represents a portrait feature the shipped product lacks. **FAIL** (same defect, counted once).
- E12 (≤10 words), E15 (frame 1 differs from H33): pass, not at issue.
- The council's optional fix ("direct Grandpa's laugh to match the photo's grin, pays 'Same grin' by picture") does **not** cure this: it makes the staged picture agree with the caption, which strengthens the implied claim instead of backing it with a product fact.

### Disagreement with the writers (recorded, not reconciled)

- Script flag 1 says "the body supports the attribute reading". True for the body (sweater, glasses, dog), but the hook caption is what carries the claim, and it is locked verbatim (E51), so the body cannot fix it.

## 2. H33 + beats 2–3 muted cold read

### Method and limit
A simulated cold read by a reader who did not build the ad (me), from the frame specs and captions only, muted. **It is not a substitute for the human cold read owed at Gate D (C6/E25/E54).**

Muted sequence as scripted (with council must-fixes 1, 2 and 4 applied):
- 0.0–2.0: hands lift a cartoon-style wooden ornament from a kraft box. "She matched it / to last year's photo."
- 2.0–6.0: a photo of the family lies beside the ornament; a fingertip goes from Grandma in the photo to printed Grandma, then from Grandpa's glasses in the photo to the printed glasses. "Grandma's red sweater. / Grandpa's round glasses."
- 6.0–8.8: photo of Rosie lifted beside printed Rosie. "Even Rosie / got a spot."

### What a stranger concludes
- Product: a personalized family ornament. **Clear** (E25 product part passes).
- **How it is personalized: ambiguous, and the likely reading is wrong.** "Matched it to [a] photo" + a photo laid beside a cartoon print + item-by-item comparison is the standard grammar of "send us your photo, we draw your family" (custom-portrait POD ads). Nothing on screen shows a choice being made: no menu, no options, no "picked". The finger-checking reads as Olivia approving a commissioned portrait as easily as matching options herself. The ornament's Pixar-style faces add to this.
- The captions name attributes (sweater, glasses), which slightly favours the right reading, and the council's copy fix ("Every detail chosen, one person at a time") helps in the primary text, but the copy is not seen in a muted autoplay and cannot fix the video read.

### Criteria
- **E25 / E54** (TEAM, S2): the story is retold, but with the wrong mechanism. **FLAG.**
- **E43 / P2 risk:** the caption is literally true (she did match it), so this is an implied misread, not an explicit false claim; hence S2, not S1. Expected cost: buyers arrive expecting a photo upload, find option pickers, and drop at the personalizer or complain after purchase.

### Is the personalizer-screen insert needed?
**Yes.** It is the only element that shows the true mechanism (choosing options) and kills the photo-to-portrait read without touching the locked hook. Conditions:
1. **Real screen recording** of the live personalizer showing the sweater or glasses picker for this cast, composited (council). No mock UI (E37 spirit; E29's "no fake UI").
2. **Placement in the shared body only** (after 3.20), so both ads stay frame-identical outside the hook window (E13). Best spot: inside the merged 2.0–6.0 STAKES shot, on the glasses step, so it shows what the caption says (E50).
3. **E32 conflict, to resolve at 04:** a standalone 1s cut breaks E32 (non-hook shots 2–5s). Make it either a phone in Olivia's hand inside the existing shot (picture-in-picture, no new cut), or a ≥2.0s shot with retimed neighbours that keeps no two consecutive shots equal. Do not add a 1s hard cut.
4. Captions must not cover the screen's picker or the printed figures (E60).
