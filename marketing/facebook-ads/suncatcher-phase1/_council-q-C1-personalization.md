# Question to the council — case C1-personalization

MadeJustForYou: small US-market Shopify print-on-demand personalized-gift store (solo operator + AI). Product: 6-inch stained-glass-style
suncatcher panel, dog silhouette cut to the chosen breed (German Shepherd) with the dog's name ("Alex") inside, .95, break-even ROAS
1.21x, target CPA $15.64. Memorial gift. All video is AI-generated (Seedance) with the real product artwork composited/Ken-Burns'd, never
drawn by the model. Checkout is verified working with real orders. Phase-1 ads are judged on hook rate (kill <18%, advance >=28%) and
link CTR (kill <1%, advance >=2.5%) at ~$20/ad, then LPV->ATC must be non-zero.

The approved test plan (authority) runs 3 concepts x 2 hooks: C1 Personalization (concept 01), C2 Reaction (concept 02), C3 Buyer swap
(concept 06) x Hook 1 "That's not a generic dog. That's his actual breed — and his name." / Hook 2 "We still leave the window open for him."
LOCKED and NOT up for debate: the concept assignment, the two hook lines, structure A, 20s, music bed no voice, proof = name+breed visible,
standard offer, CTA "Make one that's only theirs" / Shop Now, the three concepts sharing one body skeleton and differing in one place,
and the rule that the gift is identifiable in the first 3 seconds.

**The question:** Is the script for case **C1-personalization** the strongest execution of its concept inside those locks — will each of its two ads
(H1, H2) stop a cold US Facebook/Instagram scroller, make clear what is sold, make the concept's buying reason land, and drive a click?
What specifically is weak, and what concrete craft changes (shots, captions, timing, casting, prompts, copy) would you make? Also judge
the "Conflicts flagged" the writer listed for this case: which matter, which don't.
