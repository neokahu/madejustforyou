**1. Strongest: D.** It finds the core flaw: C1's own beats (B2 bowl, B4 hand) do no personalization work, so the "Personalization" cell can't be read. It also names the risk most likely to cost money: a 6-inch painted-silhouette panel won't project a glowing, legible "Alex", and the B3b caption and headline promise that it will. Best result: D's diagnosis plus C's fixes.

**2. Biggest blind spot: A.** It is a good production memo (scale, relighting, one camera move per clip), but it never asks whether the ad sells. It doesn't see that the concept slot is empty, it misses the projection honesty problem, and it waves off C1-c ("nobody checks optics"). E makes the same C1-c mistake.

**3. What all five missed:** hook rate, the first kill metric, is measured on 0–3s. That window is the shared hook clip plus the first second of B2. Every proposed fix lands after 3s. So C1 vs C2/C3 can only separate on CTR at ~$20/ad (roughly 1–2k impressions), which is too noisy to call. Either give C1 a concept-specific frame inside 2–3s, or accept that hook rate can't judge this concept.
