# Review 4 — C1-personalization

**1. Strongest: D.** It names the structural flaw. C1's own beats (bowl, hand) are grief beats, and the personalization lives only in shared footage. That means the cell can't win or lose on its concept, and a C1-vs-C2 result would be uninterpretable. Its fix, a breed/name flip, is the only proposal that shows "not a generic dog" instead of just saying it. D also catches the honesty risk that matters most for refunds: a 6-inch panel can't project a glowing, legible name. C reaches the same core diagnosis with good fixes (collar tag, photo), but it misses the physics.

**2. Biggest blind spot: A.** It's a competent production memo that never asks whether the ad sells. It doesn't check whether the death reads, whether the viewer learns "any breed," or whether the name projection is true. It dismisses C1-c ("nobody checks optics") when that is the same claim that drives "fake/AI" comments and refunds. E makes the same dismissal.

**3. What all five missed:** scale and continuity past the click. A wall-sized shadow implies a large object, and no one says to show the panel at true 6-inch size in a hand. No one checks that the landing page shows a breed picker and name preview that match what the ad promises. That matters because LPV->ATC is a gate.
