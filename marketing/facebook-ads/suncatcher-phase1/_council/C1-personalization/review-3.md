# Review 3: C1-personalization

**1. Strongest: C.** It finds the core flaw: C1's own beats (B2, B4) do grief work, not personalization work. Its two fixes stay inside the locks and each one solves more than one problem. The engraved ALEX tag plants the name before the wall payoff and gives H2 a personalization signal at 2–3s. The photo of the living dog proves the breed match, removes C1-c (no hand in the beam) and softens C1-b. D makes the same diagnosis and adds the most important risk (a 6" panel cannot project a legible name), but its breed-flip montage dilutes the single-dog story.

**2. Biggest blind spot: A.** It is a production checklist and never asks whether the ads stop a scroll, sell anything or get a click. It also dismisses C1-c ("nobody checks optics"), and so does E. That ignores the trust and refund risk D raised: the ad promises a glowing name inside the shadow, which the physical product may not deliver.

**3. What all five missed:** how the test result will be read. The kill gate (hook rate) is set by the shared first 3s, so C1's slot can only move CTR and LPV→ATC. None of them said what result would count as evidence for personalization. Also, no one checked whether the 6" product scale or "Alex" is legible at feed size with the sound off.
