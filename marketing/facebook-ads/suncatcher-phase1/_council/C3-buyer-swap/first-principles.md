What is C3 actually testing? It isn't "does this suncatcher move people." C1/C2 cover that. C3 asks whether a second buyer exists: someone who doesn't know what to send a grieving friend. The buying reason is "I need a sympathy gift that isn't flowers." Judge every frame by whether a would-be giver sees herself in it. On that test the script hides its own concept.

1. **The dilemma arrives at 12.3s.** "Flowers felt wrong" is the whole buying reason, and it sits in beat 4, after most cold viewers have gone. Beat 2 is the concept slot, so put it there: 3.0–6.0 **"Alex was my neighbor's dog. Flowers felt wrong."** Beat 4 then becomes **"This felt like Alex."** The headline already gets this right ("What to Send When Flowers Feel Wrong"). The video should say the same thing in the first 6 seconds.

2. **F-BOX reads as unboxing, which is the recipient's moment.** Hands lifting a panel out of tissue look like someone receiving a gift. Run the Ken Burns as *lowering into* the box (start tight on "Alex", pull out to the box), and PIL-paste a small handwritten tag in the box: "For Walt — Alex's window." Now the hands read as the giver's.

3. **Beat 4 lands on the wrong emotion.** The giver's fantasy isn't his grief. It's "I got it right." End the shot on him lifting his eyes from the box to *her*, with the faint smile aimed at her. Add that to Shot 2 of the Seedance prompt.

**Conflicts:**
- **C3-a matters most.** In S-C3-H2, "we" followed by "our neighbor" reads as a contradiction. The fix above removes "our" from beat 2 entirely ("my neighbor's dog"). That settles the speaker without touching the hook.
- **C3-e matters for reading the results.** C3 is the only cell with two people on screen, so any win on connection is partly structural.
- **C3-b, C3-c, C3-f don't matter.** Nobody tracks chronology in a 20s ad, and the timing and product-gap rules are both met.
- **C3-d** is required work, not a conflict.
