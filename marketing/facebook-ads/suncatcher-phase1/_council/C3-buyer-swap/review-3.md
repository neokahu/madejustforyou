# Review 3: C3-buyer-swap

**1. Strongest: D.** It names what C3 actually tests: whether a second buyer exists, the friend who doesn't know what to send. It then judges each frame against that. Its beat-2 line, "Alex was my neighbor's dog. Flowers felt wrong.", fixes three problems at once: the buying reason now arrives at 3s instead of 12.3s, it removes "our" (C3-a), and it says "dog" (C's point that "Alex" reads as a person's name). It is also the only response to see that F-BOX reads as the recipient unboxing, not the giver packing. Pair it with A's production fixes: swap one person, and cut the four-hand choreography.

**2. Biggest blind spot: A.** It is solid on production and cost but blind to persuasion. It never sees that the buying reason doesn't appear until 12.3s, the core weakness that B, C, D and E all caught. A fixes how the ad gets built, not whether it sells.

**3. What all five missed:**
- **Sympathy gifts are urgent.** None of them addresses POD lead time, shipping straight to the grieving owner, or a gift note.
- **The giver needs the dog's breed and name to order.** Nobody tackles that friction.
- **The landing page.** Its copy is written for owners, so C3 clicks may arrive with no "what to send" match. That threatens the LPV→ATC gate.
