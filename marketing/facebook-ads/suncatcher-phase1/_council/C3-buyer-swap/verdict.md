# Council verdict: C3-buyer-swap

Map (C3): A = Executor, B = Contrarian, C = Outsider, D = First Principles, E = Expansionist. All five peer reviews ranked First Principles strongest. Four of the five named Executor's response as the biggest blind spot; the fifth named Expansionist.

## Where the Council Agrees
- **The buying reason arrives too late.** Contrarian, First Principles, Expansionist and Outsider all flagged it. The line "Flowers felt wrong" *is* the concept, and it first appears at 12.3s. Until then C3 looks like C1 with a box. If C3 loses as written, the result can't separate "the concept failed" from "the caption came too late" (Contrarian). The fix fits inside the locks: beat 2 is the concept slot.
- **C3-a is real, and one word fixes it.** Four advisors want "My" in place of "Our". Only Expansionist would leave it as is.
- **C3-b doesn't matter** to a muted scroller. Contrarian alone wants a caption fix for it.
- **C3-c and C3-e are notes for reading the results**, not script changes. **C3-d** is production work.

## Where the Council Clashes
- **Beat 4, closed box or panel shown.** Executor calls the closed box correct and safe. Contrarian and Outsider say the emotional peak is "a man hugging cardboard" and want the real panel composited in. First Principles wants the shot to end on his eyes going to *her*.
- **Beat 4 choreography.** Expansionist wants the forearm touch to read clearly. Executor wants it cut, because a four-hand pass plus a touch plus an internal cut inside 5s is where Seedance morphs fingers.
- **Beat 2 wording.** Expansionist's "I didn't know what to send" drops both "dog" and "Alex". In H2 that leaves no word "dog" on screen until 9s. First Principles' "Alex was my neighbor's dog" fixes this, and also Outsider's point that "Alex" reads as a person's name.
- **C3-e.** Expansionist calls it "the finding". The others call it a confound.

## Blind Spots the Council Caught
- **F-BOX reads as unboxing**, which is the recipient's moment, not the giver's (First Principles only).
- **The F-BOX end crop leaves "Alex" about 60px wide.** That's too small to read in the 2–3s window (Executor).
- **The primary text reads as an invented testimonial** when a brand page posts it in first person (Contrarian).
- **What happens after the click** (all five reviews): ship-to-recipient, gift note, arrival date, whether the giver knows the breed, and a landing page written for owners. Any of these can zero out LPV→ATC for exactly the buyer C3 exists to find. The ad may also promise kraft-and-tissue packaging the POD doesn't ship.
- **At ~$20 per ad, H1 vs H2 within C3 is noise** (Review 4).

## The Recommendation
Use First Principles' frame: C3 tests whether a *second buyer* exists. Put the dilemma in beat 2, make the hands read as the giver's, and end beat 4 on the giver's payoff. Use Executor's production method for beat 4: swap one person on the existing frame and use a single continuous move. Show the product in beat 4 with a short composite still (the F-BOX method), so the peak reacts to Alex and not to cardboard. Every one of these changes stays inside the C3 concept slot. The skeleton is untouched.

## The One Thing to Do First
Rewrite the beat 2 caption (3.0–6.0s) to **"Alex was my neighbor's dog. / Flowers felt wrong."** It costs nothing and fixes the timing, C3-a, and "Alex = a person".

- **Gift-buyer support on the product page:** ship-to-recipient address, gift note, and a stated arrival window. Sympathy gifts are urgent. Can a giver order knowing only the breed and name?
- **Landing page for givers:** a "what to send" variant would change the test structure, so it's your decision. Otherwise C3 may win on CTR and still show 0 ATC, which would falsely read as a concept failure.
