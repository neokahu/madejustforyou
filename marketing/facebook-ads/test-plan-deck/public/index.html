MadeJustForYou · Paid video testing

Finding the hook + concept formula

A deep test plan: hooks and concepts are the two biggest levers, so each product breaks them into 13 elements across 6 rounds. Everything else stays fixed.

Budget figures assume a cost per 1,000 impressions (CPM) of $20–30 on purchase-optimized campaigns, $25 as the midpoint.

The approach

Every ad is built from tagged building blocks, so every result says why

1 · Build from codes

Concepts: angle, buyer, recipient × occasion, structure, point of view, proof, format, reveal, tone. Hooks: format × trigger + the opening visuals (frame-1 subject, motion, product and people in frame). 137 codes, 21 dimensions.

2 · Test in grids

Each ad combines several elements on purpose, so one ad answers several questions. 8 ads can measure 4 elements at once.

3 · Pool into a model

Across products, one model explains hook rate, hold rate and CTR by every code. That is the formula.

Judged on attention (hook rate, hold rate, CTR). Purchases are a guardrail against break-even, never the way to pick a winner: a fair purchase comparison costs $1,300+ each.

Terms · the ad

What an ad is made of

ConceptThe whole idea of the ad: what it says, to whom, and how the story is told.

HookThe first 1–3 seconds, which decide whether someone stops scrolling.

Frame 1The very first image of the video, before anything moves.

OnsetThe first movement or change on screen within 0.5s (camera push, light change, a hand reaching…).

Personalization timingWhen the custom detail (the pet's name, the grandkids' names) first shows: in the first second, or by ~3s.

AngleThe reason to buy the ad leads with, e.g. "keep them close" or "proof you paid attention".

Buyer roleWho the ad speaks to: a close family member, a sympathy giver, someone buying for themselves, a group.

Proof typeHow the ad shows the gift is good: the name readable on the real product, a reaction, real reviews.

CodeA short tag for one option, e.g. ON3 = light change at the start. Every ad is tagged so results can be read by code.

Strong for giftsCodes the research expects to work best for gifts; they are tested first.

Terms · the test

How results are measured and read

ImpressionsHow many times the ad was shown.

CPMCost per 1,000 impressions. The plan assumes $20–30, $25 in the middle.

Hook rateShare of impressions where someone watched at least 3 seconds. Main score for openings: below 18% = stop, 28%+ = strong.

Hold rateOf those who watched 3s, the share who reach 15s. Shows whether the rest of the ad keeps them.

CTRClick-through rate: share of impressions that led to a click through to the store.

Break-even cost per saleThe most we can pay in ads for one order without losing money (~$23–27 today).

RoundOne batch of ads launched together to answer one question (C0, H1, C1, C2, H2, B).

Ad grid8 (or 4) ads that mix two options of several elements so each option sits in half the ads; one batch measures every element.

Ad setMeta's budget container. Tests use one ad per ad set with equal budgets, so every ad gets a fair share.

Settled dayA day whose numbers Meta has finished counting; same-day numbers run high and drop later.

Run order

Four stages, about 3–4 weeks per product

1 · C0 alone

6 concepts × 2 hooks. Every later round builds on its winner.

Production: 6 concepts.

2 · H1 + C1 together

New openings on the winner, and new versions of its core, side by side.

H1: hook swaps on existing footage. C1: new scenes.

3 · C2 + H2 together

Execution grid on the winning core and opening, plus hook types.

C2: new scenes. H2: hook swaps.

4 · B

Top 2 ads against break-even cost per sale.

No new production.

Two rounds run together only when they change different parts of the ad (H1 and H2 never together), each in its own ad sets with equal budgets, launched the same day. Each round takes ~3–5 days at ~$14 per ad per day. Products overlap, so 5 products take about 2–3 months.

What "strong for gifts" means

A code is strong for gifts when it shows what the giver is really buying

The thought made visible

Givers buy proof they paid attention: the pet's real breed, the grandkids' names. Codes that show the personalization (reveal, proof on the real product) rank high.

Connection, not the object

The smile and the relationship are the benefit. Codes that show giver and recipient together, or a face reacting, rank high. A lonely giftee is never used.

Emotion that turns warm

Tender, bittersweet and joyful tones fit; humor never on a memorial. Recognition, desire and nostalgia triggers beat contrarian or urgency.

Ranks come from the research behind the frameworks (gift psychology, attention studies, our own ad audits). They are the starting hypothesis: test results can promote or demote any code.

Rules that keep the results true

Setup, stops and checks

Setup

One ad per ad set, equal daily budgets, same audience and placements, launched together. Parallel rounds get their own ad sets. Advantage+ creative off. CBO only for scaling.

Stops

C0: hook rate below 18% after ~800 impressions, kill. Grids: stop as a whole at a checkpoint. B: 3× break-even cost per sale with 0 purchases, stop. Settled days only.

Checks

A concept's result is all its ads. Judge on hook rate, hold rate and CTR; sales are a guardrail only. Correct for chance when many elements are read at once.

Every ad is named with its key (e.g. MJ4U-003_C0_K01X1-H07_E0_v01) so Meta results link back to its codes automatically in Hook Lab.
