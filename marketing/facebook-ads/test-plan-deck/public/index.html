Finding the hook + concept formula

A test plan that builds every ad from tagged building blocks, tests many elements at once, and turns 5 products into a model of what makes gift ads work.

The approach

Every ad is built from tagged building blocks, so every result says why

1 · Build from codes

Concepts: angle, buyer, recipient × occasion, structure, point of view, proof, format, reveal, tone. Hooks: format × trigger + 9 opening elements. 137 codes, 21 dimensions.

2 · Test in grids

Each ad combines several elements on purpose, so one ad answers several questions. 8 ads can measure 4 elements at once.

3 · Pool into a model

Across products, one model explains hook rate, hold rate and CTR by every code. That is the formula.

Judged on attention (hook rate, hold rate, CTR). Purchases are a guardrail against break-even, never the way to pick a winner: a fair purchase comparison costs $1,300+ each.

How it reads

Each element is compared 4 ads vs 4 ads, about 6,800 impressions per side.

That detects gaps of about 2 points of hook rate, for all 4 elements at once.

Trade-off: it measures each element alone. If two only work together, a small follow-up confirms it.

Rules that keep the results true

Setup, stops and checks

Setup

One ad per ad set, equal daily budgets, same audience and placements, launched together. Purchase campaigns otherwise starve some ads. CBO only for scaling.

Stops

Hook rate below 18% after ~800 impressions: kill. A concept spending 2.5× break-even cost per sale with 0 purchases: stop. Settled days only.

Checks

A concept's result is all its ads. Correct for chance when ~20 elements are read at once. Codes that always change together are read as a bundle.

Every ad is named with its key (e.g. MJ4U-003_R0_K01X1-H07_E0_v01) so Meta results link back to its codes automatically in Hook Lab.
